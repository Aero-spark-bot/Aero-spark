1. What Is Space Exploration?

Space exploration is the investigation of outer space using satellites, telescopes, robotic spacecraft and human missions.

Scientists explore space to understand the universe, study planets, discover new worlds and learn more about Earth's place in the cosmos.

Space is full of stars, planets, galaxies, nebulae and many objects that scientists are still discovering.

2. Our Solar System

Our solar system contains the Sun, eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids and comets.

☿ Mercury

The smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun.

♀ Venus

A very hot planet with a thick atmosphere.

🌍 Earth

Our home planet, known to support life.

♂ Mars

The red planet, explored by several robotic missions.

♃ Jupiter

The largest planet in our solar system.

♄ Saturn

Famous for its spectacular ring system.

♅ Uranus

An ice giant that rotates on a very tilted axis.

♆ Neptune

A distant ice giant with extremely fast winds.

3. Stars and Galaxies

What Is a Star?

A star is a massive, luminous ball of hot plasma that produces energy through nuclear fusion. The Sun is the nearest star to Earth.

What Is a Galaxy?

A galaxy is a huge collection of stars, gas, dust and dark matter held together by gravity.

Milky Way

Our home galaxy, containing our solar system.

Andromeda

A large galaxy that is relatively close to the Milky Way.

Nebulae

Vast clouds of gas and dust found in space.

4. Artificial Satellites

Artificial satellites are human-made objects placed into orbit around Earth or another celestial body.

Uses of Satellites

  • Weather forecasting and climate monitoring.
  • Communication, television and internet services.
  • Navigation and positioning systems.
  • Earth observation and environmental research.
  • Scientific experiments and space exploration.

5. Human Space Exploration

Humans have travelled beyond Earth to study space and conduct scientific experiments.

International Space Station

A research laboratory in low Earth orbit where astronauts conduct experiments.

Apollo Moon Missions

NASA's Apollo programme achieved the first human Moon landing in 1969.

Future Moon Missions

New missions aim to expand human exploration of the Moon.

6. Famous Space Missions

Voyager 1

A NASA spacecraft launched in 1977 that explored the outer planets and entered interstellar space.

Hubble Space Telescope

A space telescope that has helped scientists study distant galaxies and cosmic objects.

Chandrayaan-3

India's lunar mission that achieved a successful soft landing near the Moon's south polar region in 2023.

Mangalyaan

India's Mars Orbiter Mission, which successfully entered Mars orbit in 2014.

James Webb Space Telescope

A powerful space observatory designed to study the early universe, stars and exoplanets.

Aditya-L1

India's solar observatory mission, designed to study the Sun.

7. How Do We Travel Through Space?

Spacecraft use rocket engines to gain the speed needed to travel through space. Their journeys are carefully planned using mathematics, physics and orbital mechanics.

Important Concepts

  • Launch: A rocket lifts a spacecraft from a launch site.
  • Orbit: The spacecraft follows a curved path around a celestial body.
  • Gravity Assist: A spacecraft uses a planet's gravity and motion to change its speed and direction.
  • Landing: A spacecraft reaches the surface of a planet, moon or other body.

8. Important Space Science Formulas

Universal Gravitation

F = G × (m₁ × m₂) / r²

F = gravitational force, G = gravitational constant, m₁ and m₂ = masses, and r = distance between their centres.

Orbital Speed for a Circular Orbit

v = √(GM / r)

v = orbital speed, G = gravitational constant, M = mass of the central body, and r = orbital radius.

Light-Year

1 light-year ≈ 9.46 trillion kilometres

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year. It is a unit of distance, not time.

9. Search for Life Beyond Earth

Scientists study Mars, icy moons and planets orbiting distant stars to investigate whether environments suitable for life may exist elsewhere.

  • Exoplanets: Planets orbiting stars outside our solar system.
  • Europa: A moon of Jupiter that may contain a subsurface ocean.
  • Enceladus: A moon of Saturn with water-rich plumes.
  • Mars: A planet studied for evidence of ancient water and possible past habitability.
Scientists have not yet confirmed life beyond Earth.
